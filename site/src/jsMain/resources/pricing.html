WARM / COLD

Why is my pour cold?

Because the bar is quiet. Every tap is its own GPU, and a GPU nobody is using gets switched off. Taps scale to zero when the bar is quiet, so a cold pour takes about 30 seconds. After that the tap stays warm for a minute past your last message. Reply inside the minute and it stays warm. Take longer and the next pour is cold again: another wait, and the cold minimum.

A cold tap means not enough people are drinking here yet.

While you read and type, the tap you woke is sitting warm and already paid for. The next drinker on that tap shares it, and rides on it for nearly nothing. Two drinkers on one tap: the pour costs us about half. Four: about a quarter. That is the whole trick, and it is the only one.

More drinkers, warmer taps, cheaper pours. For everyone.

So drink from the house pours. They are marked. They are the taps most likely to be warm. Every drinker who follows the crowd makes the pour cheaper for the crowd. LAST CALL is the schedule that hands it back.

Minimum pour.

Every pour holds a tap for a minute, so every pour costs at least a minute: 1.2 / 1.5 / 2.5 cents by shelf. Waking a cold tap costs about double: 2.5 / 3 / 5 cents. Reply within a minute with a normal-sized context and you will not notice either. Short prompts, or long pauses between messages, and you will.

Taps are shared. Each tap is one GPU running one model; a few members may be served by the same tap at the same time. That sharing is what lowers the cost.

STRAIGHT ANSWERS

Is it open?
No. Nothing is for sale. The prices are the prices at opening, our best guess at cost. Open a tab and we write when the doors open.
Is this the cheapest way to run an uncensored model?
No. If you chat for hours a day, or run 16k of context all evening, a flat-rate host will cost you less and you should go there. Small models especially are cheaper almost anywhere else. We are for people who want these exact bottles, on a tap shared with a handful of drinkers instead of a crowd, from scripts and apps as well as chat, and who would rather see a meter than a queue.
What does abliterated mean?
A model whose refusals were cut out after training. Abliteration is one way to do it; the maker's Heretic pass is another; some of the merges are simply built from uncensored parts. Not every bottle had the same treatment. Each card prints what that bottle's own label says, and links to the maker's page.
Who makes these?
One builder makes nine of the ten; the guest tap is from another cellar. Each card links to the maker's own page. We are not affiliated with any of them.
Can I point scripts and apps at it?
Yes. It is an API. The tab and the per-pass request limits are the limit.
Why does a long chat drain faster than a short one?
Your client resends the whole conversation each turn and we bill it as input each turn. Trim the context and the tab lasts longer.
Why did my tab move when I only said hello?
Minimum pour. Every pour holds a tap for a minute, so every pour costs at least a minute. See WARM / COLD.
Do thinking models cost more?
The thinking is output. It is billed as output, including thinking your client hides. Six of the ten taps think: mini, reason, the three 27Bs and the flagship.
Why did I wait 30 seconds?
The tap was cold. Taps scale to zero when the bar is quiet, and a tap goes cold 60 seconds after its last request. The sign shows which taps are warm.
Does my unused tab roll over?
No. Rounds you bought do, for a year.
Do I get a tap to myself?
No. Taps are shared. Each tap is one GPU running one model, and a few members may be served by it at the same time. That sharing is what lowers the cost.
What do you log?
This page: what the Privacy page says, and no more. The API: nothing is promised here. Its policy gets written down before the doors open. Until it is, assume nothing.
Is this an adult site?
No. It is a fiction-writing and developer API, 18 and over, with an acceptable-use policy. Read it.
